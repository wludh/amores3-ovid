# Vocabulary sources and lookup policy

The poem glossaries in `Companion/Vocab/` remain archived unchanged. The reader applies the witness-specific corrections in `vocabulary-review/` before showing them. Expanded help adds definitions from the **Dickinson College Commentaries Latin Core Vocabulary**, checked against readable forms in the selected witness. Neither this lookup nor the slider changes the TEI text.

## DCC vocabulary

Christopher Francese and the Dickinson College Commentaries team, [Latin Core Vocabulary](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/latin-core-list1), downloaded 26 October 2026. The [DCC methodology and reuse page](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/vocab/core-vocabulary) describes the list and its frequency sources. The list is licensed [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). `latin-frequency.json` preserves the published definitions and ranks; its source metadata and `scripts/sources/vocabulary/dcc-latin.csv` record the download. The present official export contains 997 entries, under the familiar “DCC 1000” name.

DCC ranks apply to the core list. Words outside it do not receive invented numerical ranks. “All words” in the tool means all available glossary words and supported dictionary lookups, not a promise of complete lexical analysis of every witness token.

## Morphological lookup

`vocabulary-forms.json` is generated by `scripts/build-vocabulary-lexicon.py` using [LatinCy lexicon](https://github.com/latincy/latincy-lexicon), pinned to commit `4a12d066e5800c941e472fa5af610f156eb022f1`. Its rule-based analyzer and form generator use William Whitaker's WORDS dictionary and inflection tables. The Python code is MIT-licensed; Whitaker's original data allow use for any purpose. The complete notice is preserved in `scripts/sources/vocabulary/latincy-whitaker-LICENSE.txt`. This build does not use LatinCy's statistical language models or its Lewis and Short sense store.

The lookup normalizes only dictionary keys: case and combining accents, `v/u`, `j/i`, `æ/ae`, `œ/oe`, and long `ſ/s`. The reader still sees the witness's spelling. No fuzzy spelling correction or substitution from another witness is performed.

A candidate must be an actual form in the library's generated paradigm, not just a match to an unconstrained stem and ending. Duplicate DCC headwords are distinguished by dictionary part of speech. Explicit Whitaker participle references (for example, *iens* as the present participle of *eo*) are followed; its documented internal pronoun headword `h` is mapped to *hic* only for pronoun analyses. Complete forms printed in a DCC headword may supply a direct match when morphological analysis yields no candidate.

Two restrictions prevent known analyzer overgeneration. [Allen and Greenough §182](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/peculiarities) limits the zero-vowel imperative to *dic, duc, fac, fer* and the appropriate compounds. [§190](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/deponent-verbs) supplies the passive morphology of finite deponents. The build therefore rejects unsupported bare-stem imperatives and finite active analyses attached solely to a deponent entry; an independently listed active lexical counterpart remains eligible. Participles and infinitives are not removed by that finite-form restriction. Historical or unrecognized forms can remain without an added lookup.

A single mapping requires one viable lexical lemma and one DCC entry. If every remaining candidate belongs to DCC but more than one entry remains, `ambiguousForms` preserves the candidate set for contextual review. For example, the unreviewed lookup for *est* contains both *sum* and *edo*. This morphological agreement alone does not establish the correct lemma or sense in a verse. The contextual review below must resolve or suppress the proposed entry before it reaches the reader.

Readable source forms are collected only from the 75 explicitly enrolled P, Y, S, O, and LL poem–witness files in `scripts/sources/vocabulary/tei-corpus.json`. This is the exact corpus snapshot used for the lexical review, preserved from commit `6f80458`. The builder verifies that each enrolled witness/poem still has the same path and reviewed status in the live corpus. Additive live records do not enter the lookup: the newly transcribed W witness remains available as text, but its vocabulary needs its own contextual review. Enrollment, transcription review, and contextual vocabulary review are separate checks.

A word touching an abbreviation, uncertainty, glyph, gap, deletion, source mark, or supplied text is omitted from automatic lookup. The dictionary also analyzes the initial forms of existing glossary headwords for core-list classification. Editorial notes and other non-verse prose never supply vocabulary tokens. Source choices use their written `abbr`/`orig`/`sic` branch, and unsafe written abbreviations remain excluded.

## Contextual vocabulary review

Lexical corrections consult Charlton T. Lewis and Charles Short, *A Latin Dictionary* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1879), digital edition by the [Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/) (1997-10-28), available from the [official Perseus lexica repository](https://github.com/PerseusDL/lexica/blob/master/CTS_XML_TEI/perseus/pdllex/lat/ls/lat.ls.perseus-eng2.xml). The digital edition is licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Derived corrective glosses retain that attribution and license; individual entry citations accompany the decisions. The original dictionary was not modified. Download provenance is recorded in `scripts/sources/vocabulary/lewis-short-source.json`.

The per-poem JSON files in `vocabulary-review/` record OpenAI Codex's review of every proposed glossary entry and dictionary match against each witness's actual verse text. Decisions identify the witness, canonical line, exact original glossary string or source form, quoted evidence, dictionary references, and a disposition: confirm, replace, or suppress. Reports in `scripts/output/vocabulary-review-*.md` explain the corrections and limitations.

Legacy errors are corrected in this overlay, preserving the original glossary files for comparison. A contextual homograph can explicitly be marked noncore even when another sense shares a DCC headword: the interjection *ā* does not inherit the frequency rank of the preposition *ā*. Unsupported word matches and irrecoverable reading uncertainty are suppressed. An entry without a matching witness-specific decision is not displayed.

Each review binds SHA-256 hashes of the witness XML, original poem glossary, DCC frequency data, and morphology lookup. The browser checks these hashes before applying the review. `test/vocabulary-review.test.mjs` regenerates candidate coverage from every enrolled TEI, checks every disposition and evidence quotation, rejects duplicates and missing decisions, and verifies that the default excludes DCC core words. Separate tests read every newer live witness and require zero vocabulary entries, including at maximum help, until it is explicitly enrolled and reviewed. These automated checks verify review coverage and integrity; they do not perform the contextual reading themselves.

After changing a bound input, inspect the changed vocabulary against the affected witness text, update the review evidence and dispositions, and rerun the tests. Rebuilding the morphology artifact alone does not renew a contextual review or a TEI transcription seal.

The release integration moved the original corpus input to the enrollment snapshot without changing its bytes. A complete rebuild was checked for exact equality of normalization, forms, ambiguous forms, excluded analyses, and coverage statistics. Only the morphology provenance changed, so only its derived `formsSha256` binding was updated in existing contextual reviews; no review decision or source binding was rewritten. The equality evidence and scope rationale are recorded in `scripts/output/vocabulary-release-integration.md`.

## Rebuilding

Use Python 3.11 or later. The morphology library has no required third-party Python dependencies.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/latincy/latincy-lexicon.git /tmp/amores-latincy-lexicon
git -C /tmp/amores-latincy-lexicon checkout 4a12d066e5800c941e472fa5af610f156eb022f1
python3.11 scripts/build-vocabulary-lexicon.py --lexicon-source /tmp/amores-latincy-lexicon
npm test
```

Rebuild the DCC data first if its source CSV changes. The generated artifact records SHA-256 digests for the generator, dictionary files, frequency dataset, explicit corpus enrollment snapshot, every input transcription, and every existing glossary. The tests detect stale inputs and changed live enrollment records while allowing additional witnesses outside vocabulary scope. The process writes only `vocabulary-forms.json`; it does not transcribe text, alter the scholarly glossary, modify annotation rectangles, or reseal a TEI review.
